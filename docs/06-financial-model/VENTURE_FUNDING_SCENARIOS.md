# Venture funding scenarios

**Private transaction planning model · 20 November 2026 · Nominal Brazilian reais (BRL)**

Three hypothetical primary equity financings fund different stages of PoWV development. Each could be the last round. The ownership and price calculations below show what would happen if all three closed in sequence. The amounts are planning assumptions. PoWV has announced no round, agreed valuation, investor commitment, guaranteed return or redemption date.

## 1. Investment, price and ownership

| Round and funded objective | Delivery window from that closing | New cash | Assumed stake sold at full funding | Implied pre-money | Implied post-money | Price per model unit |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A · Independent validation of one capability | 4 months | 150,000 | 15% | 850,000 | 1,000,000 | 1.00 |
| B · Pilot with one operating partner | 8 months | 500,000 | 20% | 2,000,000 | 2,500,000 | 2.00 |
| C · Productization and replication | 12 months | 1,250,000 | 20% | 5,000,000 | 6,250,000 | 4.00 |

The model starts with 850,000 founder units. These units express economic ownership for comparison. Legal quota counts, nominal capital and accounting entries depend on the issuer and closing documents. Each round adds cash to the company. The model assumes one new investor per round, no founder sale, no options, convertibles or other claims, and no follow-on purchases by existing holders.

**Pricing method.** Post-money = new cash ÷ stake sold. Pre-money = post-money − new cash. Unit price = pre-money ÷ units outstanding immediately before closing. A, B and C would issue 150,000, 250,000 and 312,500 new units respectively. The assumed stakes produce the prices. Neither the price nor either 100% step-up comes from a market appraisal or PoWV transaction. The BRL 148,275 historical development-cost scenario measures a different quantity.

| Ownership after full funding | Founder | A investor | B investor | C investor |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| After A | 85.0% | 15.0% | — | — |
| After B | 68.0% | 12.0% | 20.0% | — |
| After C | 54.4% | 9.6% | 16.0% | 20.0% |

**Price sensitivity.** If B raised the same BRL 500,000 at A's BRL 1.00 unit price, B would receive 33.33%, the founder 56.67% and A 10.00% immediately after B. A successful technical review would give the parties evidence for a negotiation. It would create no automatic right to a higher price.

### US dollar view and 12-month currency sensitivity

**Reference date: 20 November 2026.** The latest available closing PTAX selling rate was **BRL 5.8889 per USD on 19 November**. USD equivalent = fixed BRL amount ÷ BRL per USD. Figures are rounded to whole dollars before bank spreads, transfer charges and applicable taxes. [Central Bank of Brazil closing PTAX](https://ptax.bcb.gov.br/ptax_internet/consultarUltimaCotacaoDolar.do).

| Round | New cash (BRL) | USD at 5.8889 | USD at 5.30 | USD at 6.48 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 150,000 | 25,472 | 28,302 | 23,148 |
| B | 500,000 | 84,906 | 94,340 | 77,160 |
| C | 1,250,000 | 212,264 | 235,849 | 192,901 |

At the reference rate the illustrative **pre-money → post-money** values in USD are **144,339 → 169,811** for A, **339,622 → 424,528** for B and **849,055 → 1,061,319** for C. Translation leaves the modeled ownership percentages unchanged.

**Outlook through November 2027.** The Focus survey published on 16 November reported market medians of **5.40 for December 2026 and January 2027**, and **5.45 for December 2027**. The survey predates the 19 November close. December 2027 is a year-end comparison just beyond the planning horizon. The 5.30 and 6.48 columns are hypothetical stress tests for payments over the next twelve months. Actual rates may move outside them. [13 November Focus Market Readout](https://www.bcb.gov.br/content/focus/focusmarketreadout/R20261113.pdf).

**Staged funding.** For a BRL-denominated round, the dollar outlay is the sum of each BRL installment divided by its own settlement rate. For A, BRL 60,000 at 5.8889 costs about USD 10,189. If both later installments (BRL 52,500 and 37,500) settle at 5.30, the total rises to **USD 27,170**, versus **USD 25,472** with all three at the reference rate. For a fixed USD commitment, the BRL available to the project instead varies at each closing. The agreement would specify denomination, rate source and fixing date, non-business-day convention, fees and who covers any BRL shortfall. USD returns also depend on the exchange rate when proceeds are distributed.

The engineering line uses 4, 12 and 30 person-months at BRL 16,250 per month, the 2026 national median for a senior full-stack developer reported in the Robert Half guide. Person-months measure team effort across the delivery window. Embedded engineering, security work and external evaluation would need role-specific quotes before contracting. All other lines are planning allowances, including employer charges. Actual hiring arrangements, taxes, vendor prices and travel would be costed at closing. The model assumes no historical-cost reimbursement. ## 3. Deliverables and capital release

The model assumes three separately documented closings at the agreed price for each round. The first installment funds the initial work. Each later installment requires written acceptance of the preceding milestone. Only cash actually funded counts toward the investor's modeled ownership. The 40% / 35% / 25% split and acceptance periods below are proposed contract terms.

| Round | 40% at closing | 35% after intermediate acceptance | 25% after final technical or commercial acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | **60,000** upon execution, approved budget, access to the required IP and a signed test plan naming an external evaluator. | **52,500** after delivery of a versioned test package, expected outcomes and an internally repeatable run for the chosen use case. | **37,500** after the external evaluator reproduces the agreed cases and signs a report recording results and exceptions against the agreed threshold. |
| B | **200,000** upon a partner-signed pilot scope covering baseline, data access, responsibilities, duration and success threshold. | **175,000** after installation in the agreed partner environment and acceptance of the first end-to-end sample under the pilot protocol. | **125,000** after partner sign-off on the baseline comparison, exception log and agreed acceptance threshold. A renewal remains a separate commercial decision. |
| C | **500,000** upon presentation of an accepted pilot, a signed paid contract and a costed plan for a second deployment. | **437,500** after delivery of a versioned release, deployment instructions and acceptance tests at the second site or deployment. | **312,500** after contractual acceptance at that deployment, evidence of a first customer payment and a report on actual delivery cost and gross margin. |

The test plan sets the metric, minimum result, sample, observation period and authorized reviewer before funds move. A physical capture pilot could measure time per reconciled lot. An authorization pilot could measure decision accuracy and false holds on agreed cases. The selected workflow determines the test. A commercially unsuccessful trial can still produce a valid technical report.

## 4. Budget governance and contractual protections

| Decision | Proposed authority and record |
| --- | --- |
| Approve the initial allocation | The issuer and investor sign a budget annex by cost center, milestone, owner and maximum amount. The administering company receives funds in its own account and maintains a dedicated project ledger. |
| Spend within budget | The company administrator approves ordinary expenditure within the released balance and line ceiling. Payments require a contract, invoice or payroll record. A person receiving a payment cannot approve it alone. |
| Transfer budget or use the reserve | Management may move up to 5% of the **original source line**, cumulatively, between operating lines, with a written reason. Larger transfers, any reserve release, related-party payments and changes in scope require prior written approval by the designated investor representative and a disinterested company decision maker. Statutory shareholder approvals still apply. |
| Accept a milestone | The named technical reviewer signs the evidence package. The company administrator and investor representative authorize the next closing. The example review period is 10 business days. An objection identifies the failed criterion and supporting evidence. |
| Resolve a missed milestone | Unreleased capital stays with the investor. The company has 20 business days to propose or complete a cure under the agreed terms. An unresolved technical dispute goes to a jointly appointed independent reviewer. The parties may amend scope in writing or cancel future closings under the agreement. Previously issued interests and company cash are treated under the signed documents. |
| Report and protect capital | A monthly report shows opening cash, receipts, actual and committed spending by line, variances, remaining cash and milestone status. Reserved matters include new senior financing, disposal of core IP and material related-party contracts. The documents set consent rights, voting thresholds and a deadlock process. |

The modeled economic terms are ordinary equity with pro rata exit proceeds, no liquidation preference, no fixed interest, no founder guarantee and no minimum repayment. Investor information rights, future participation rights, transfer restrictions, tag-along rights and exit approvals would be documented. A preference, option pool or anti-dilution clause would change the example and require a new calculation.

| 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

## 6. Brazilian contracting assumptions

| Stage | Contracting party and investor rights |
| --- | --- |
| Current structure | The founder operates as a Brazilian individual microentrepreneur (MEI). The MEI cannot admit an equity partner. |
| First commercial contract or pilot | The founder plans to move to a Brazilian limited liability company (*sociedade limitada* or Ltda.) as commercial work begins. With the founder as sole owner, it would be a single-member Ltda. (SLU). Microenterprise (ME) status depends on eligibility. The pilot agreement would name the operator responsible for delivery and identify its rights to use the technology. |
| Direct equity investment | The operating Ltda. would receive the cash and issue quotas to the investor. Once the investor joins the founder as a quota holder, the Ltda. has two owners. For staged capital increases, article 1,081 addresses payment in full of the existing quotas, existing holders’ pre-emption rights and amendment of the company agreement. |
| Possible future IP holding company | Subject to legal and tax review, a holding company could own specified IP and license it to an operating subsidiary. The subsidiary would contract with customers and could grant sublicenses within the agreed scope. The modeled percentages apply to the operating company. A later reorganization would require a revised ownership table and review of investor consent rights. A holding-company stake or security over IP would require a separate agreement. |
