# Capital Roadmap

**PoWV Protocol · Technology capital formation and enterprise development · 2026–2030**

PoWV’s next capital decision is which technical capability merits independent testing with a partner. The longer-term objective is to turn verified capabilities into contracts and use the cash those contracts generate to sustain operations, reward contributors and finance further research. The 2026–2030 horizon is a planning period. Funding amounts, dates and transaction terms remain open.

## Starting point

The public record includes a controlled [physical acquisition experiment](https://github.com/powvprotocol-org/powv-scale-edge-integration/blob/main/docs/integration-module/experimental-record.md), separate [software test results](https://github.com/powvprotocol-org/powv-lab-status/blob/main/status/2026-11-software-engineering-update.md) and a [historical development-cost model](../06-financial-model/TECHNOLOGY_CAPITAL_FORMATION.md). These records give prospective partners a starting point for diligence. Each application, from industrial traceability to treasury authorization, requires evidence specific to the decision it supports.

## Proposed milestones

| Stage | Work the capital would support | Evidence for the next decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Technology and evidence | Organize research, engineering records, laboratory assets and IP documentation. | A reviewer can trace each claim to a dated source and distinguish founder declarations, estimates, internal tests and independent findings. |
| 2. Independent validation and commercial readiness | An external evaluator repeats a defined test. In parallel, the project identifies who owns the relevant IP, who could license it and who would be accountable for delivery. | A report records the setup, result and limits. The parties can identify the contracting entity and any rights still to be secured. |
| 3. Commercial opportunity | A prospective partner identifies a decision that better evidence could improve, its operational owner and its current cost or performance. | A baseline and a measurable outcome give both parties a basis for selecting a [pilot](../09-commercial-strategy/README.md). |
| 4. Pilot scope and financing | The parties price the engineering, integration and evaluation work. They agree on deliverables, funding responsibilities, data rights, confidentiality, risks and acceptance criteria. | A costed proposal identifies who pays for each milestone and how acceptance would be determined. Possible routes include a customer contract, grant, research partnership or strategic investment. |
| 5. Commercial validation and revenue | The partner compares pilot results with the baseline. If the benefit justifies the price, a license, integration agreement, service contract or wider deployment could follow. | Customer acceptance, signed terms and delivery costs allow an assessment of revenue, margin and the prospects for repeat business. |
| 6. Governance and capital allocation | As operations grow, the enterprise defines authority over spending, licensing, reserves and contractual obligations. Individual operations may need separate management and financial accountability. | Identified decision makers, budgets and reporting obligations establish who controls capital and bears each exposure. |
| 7. Reinvestment and expansion | Available cash could support further engineering, academic partnerships, complementary ventures, joint ventures or acquisitions. | Each opportunity has a defined purpose, cost, responsible operator and review of its effect on existing commitments. |

## How capital would circulate

Customer receipts would first support delivery, operating costs, contractual obligations and reserves. Cash available after those needs could fund additional development or distributions under the applicable agreements. Product lines could share technology while retaining distinct contracts, responsibilities and revenue models.

Ownership and economic participation would depend on documented rights, contributions and risks. Equity, performance compensation, revenue sharing and IP royalties are possible arrangements. Retained earnings, strategic equity and non-dilutive funding could finance different stages. Any equity proposal would identify the issuing entity, its commercialization rights, investor protections and who can authorize the transaction. [Investment policy](../05-investment-policy/README.md) · [Capital structure](../04-capital-structure/README.md)

Over the longer term, financial capacity could also support scientific fellowships, education, community initiatives and non-repayable philanthropic grants. Each would require a dedicated budget and an accountable decision maker.

## Financial scope

The historical model estimates the resources used to develop the technology. A future funding request would separately identify the deliverable, capital expenditure, operating costs, commercialization costs and payment schedule. Enterprise and IP valuations require their own supporting evidence. This roadmap announces no financing round, pilot budget, restructuring, deployment commitment, investor return or liquidity event.
